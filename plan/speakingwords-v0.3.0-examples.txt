speakingwords — v0.3.0 before/after examples
=============================================

Companion to plan/speakingwords-v0.3.0.md. Every "before" in examples 1–2 was
run through the shipped skill/scripts/lint.py; every "after" lints clean.
Example 3 illustrates the proposed STE register (not yet built). Example 4 is

Example 1 — a real hook bounce
------------------------------

Before (unstyled reply, 120 words — bounced with 14 measured violations,
exit 2: strip-great-question, strip-happy-to, strip-dive-into, strip-leverage,
strip-seamless, strip-robust, strip-worth-noting, strip-whilst,
strip-it-should-be-noted, strip-in-order-to, strip-prior-to,
strip-let-me-know, strip-hope-this-helps, conc-in-other-words):

    Great question! I'd be happy to explain what's going on here. Let me dive
    into the config to understand the structure.

    So, prior to the deployment it should be noted that the migration must be
    applied first, whilst the read replicas remain in a lagging state. In
    order to avoid downtime, we leverage a robust blue-green strategy that
    seamlessly shifts traffic. In other words, the old pods keep serving until
    the new ones pass their health checks.

    It's worth noting that the timeout might possibly be a little too low in
    some cases. To summarize, run the migration, then deploy, and the lag
    should clear on its own.

    I hope this helps! Let me know if you need anything else!

After, convo voice (lints clean):

    Run the migration before you deploy. The read replicas are still lagging.
    To avoid downtime the deploy is blue-green: the old pods keep serving
    until the new ones pass their health checks. The health-check timeout may
    be too low. Run the migration, then deploy, and the lag clears on its own.

After, terse voice — same facts, different shape (lints clean):

    - Run the migration first, then deploy.
    - Read replicas are still lagging.
    - Deploy is blue-green: old pods keep serving until the new ones pass
      health checks.
    - Health-check timeout may be too low.
    - Lag clears on its own after deploy.

The cut lands past the conciseness band on purpose: nearly half the before was
strip-rule matter, and strip removal is mandatory regardless of band. The band
governs the prose that remains — Example 2 shows that.


Example 2 — the conciseness dial, in-band (counts verified)
-----------------------------------------------------------

Before (65 words of padded, violation-free prose):

    So basically what is happening here is that the cache entry is written
    with a 60-second TTL. Once that TTL expires, what happens is that the
    next request misses and goes to the database. That means, in effect, that
    a burst of traffic right after expiry can all miss at once, which is the
    thundering-herd problem, and that is why we added the request-coalescing
    lock.

low — 52 words, a 20% cut (band 10–20%). Decoration only; "what happens is
that" survives because the budget was spent:

    The cache entry is written with a 60-second TTL. Once that TTL expires,
    what happens is that the next request misses and goes to the database. A
    burst of traffic right after expiry can all miss at once, which is the
    thundering-herd problem, and that is why we added the request-coalescing
    lock.

high — 43 words, a 34% cut (band 25–35%). Every fact survives, including the
why of the lock:

    The cache entry has a 60-second TTL. When it expires, the next request
    misses and goes to the database. A traffic burst right after expiry can
    all miss at once — the thundering-herd problem — which is why we added
    the request-coalescing lock.


Example 3 — the proposed STE register (v0.3.0, not yet built)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Before (the current Slack register at its best — a correct v0.2.0 reply,
nothing bounces):

    Don't restart the pump until the pressure's back under 30 psi — if you
    restart it early, the seal can fail and you'll be looking at a full
    teardown. Once it's under 30, restart it and watch the gauge for a couple
    of minutes.

After, register: ste — no contractions, every sentence ≤25 words, active
voice, one instruction per sentence, articles kept:

    Do not restart the pump until the pressure is below 30 psi. If you
    restart the pump too early, the seal can fail. A failed seal makes a full
    teardown necessary. When the pressure is below 30 psi, restart the pump.
    Then monitor the gauge for two minutes.

Register must be a swap, not a layer: the Slack register encourages the
contractions STE forbids.


Example 4 — what mode: both puts in context (real renderer output)
------------------------------------------------------------------

Rendered by lib/memory.js renderBlock() at convo + high. This is the block
that mode: both writes into CLAUDE.md; because it is already in context every
session, both skips the SessionStart injector (the no-duplication rule):

    <!-- speakingwords:start -->
    <!-- speakingwords v0.2.0 · memory mode · voice: convo · conciseness: high · managed block, edits here are overwritten -->
    - Write like a colleague in a Slack DM. Short sentences, everyday words,
      contractions where they read naturally. Technical terms stay — it is
      the grammar around them that stays simple, never the vocabulary of the
      domain.
    - Lead with the answer. The first line resolves the question; reasoning
      comes after.
    - No self-narration. Do not say what you are about to do. Do it, then say
      what you found.
    - No filler at the edges. Never rate the question or the user; end on the
      last piece of content.
    - Concrete over vague. Replace intensifiers with the fact behind them,
      and use at most one hedge per claim.
    - Never use these words and phrases: (39 phrases, derived live from the
      lexicon strip table).
    - Voice is convo: prose is retained and brevity is not forced. Use point
      form only where the content is genuinely a list.
    - Conciseness is high: every sentence earns its place. Keep explanations,
      cut elaborations.
    - Report what a change does, not the parts it is made of. Keep the count
      and a pointer — a file, a table, a diff — and drop the roll call.
      Numbers, paths, code blocks and caveats always stay.
    <!-- speakingwords:end -->

In both mode this block is the prevention layer; the Stop hook from Example 1
fires only when the model drifts anyway. E12 proves the block earns its place:
fewer bounces with it than without. At STE the first bullet swaps for the STE
line, so the 9-line budget (assertion A1) holds without spending a line.
