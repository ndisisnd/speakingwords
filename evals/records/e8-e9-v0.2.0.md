**Both evals FAIL.** Four of the five gates across E8 and E9 are missed, and they
miss for one shared reason: the rewriter turns almost every reply into a bullet
list. Bullets cut roughly half the words whatever conciseness level is asked for,
which pushes `low` and `med` far past their bands, and a bulleted list reads to the
judge as a report rather than a Slack message, which is where the E9 register axis
loses most of its points. Nothing here has been massaged; the numbers are as

| Level | Target band | Median cut | In band |
|---|---|---|---|
| `low` | 10–20% | 47.7% | **FAIL** |
| `med` | 25–35% | 51.2% | **FAIL** |
| `high` | 40–50% | 49.7% | PASS |

Only `high` lands. `low` overshoots its 10–20% band by more than double and `med`
overshoots 25–35% by about 16 points. The three medians sit within four points of
each other (47.7%, 51.2%, 49.7%), so the level dial produced no measurable change in
output length at all — the rewriter compresses to the same place whatever it is told.

### Judged fact loss

Each of the 60 before/after pairs was judged on the single question of whether every
fact, number, path, command and code block survived, with the one documented
exemption for a retrievable enumeration collapsed to function + count + pointer.
The gate is zero losses. **5 losses were found**, so the gate fails.

- **e01_retry-budget.txt** at `high` — The original states parked jobs wait for a human to review them, but the rewrite only says the job is 'parked in queue/dead' without stating that human review is the reason it's waiting there — that fact is dropped, not just compressed.
- **e13_memory-growth.txt** at `med` — The original states each distinct requested URL adds a cache entry; the rewrite only says 'entries pile up forever' without mentioning that URLs are the specific mechanism/key driving growth, dropping that detail.
- **e16_error-shape.txt** at `med` — The rewrite drops the original's causal claim that the inconsistent error shape is why client code has to do so much branching — that consequence/reason is not retrievable from the bullet list.
- **e17_perf-regression.txt** at `high` — The original notes buffering is 'simpler to reason about' as the tradeoff motivating the switch; the rewrite drops this characterization entirely, keeping only the memory-holding fact.
- **e19_dependency-pin.txt** at `med` — The rewrite drops the specific detail that the dependency is pinned to an exact version rather than a version range, only saying 'Pin is deliberate' without that distinction.

None of the five is an enumeration case. Each is a dropped clause the reader cannot
re-derive: a reason, a mechanism, a consequence, a trade-off, or a distinction
between an exact pin and a range.

### convo + high stays prose

All 20 `convo` / `high` rewrites were re-linted for `terse-prose-block`. No
violations, so the guardrail that `high` is not a licence to collapse convo into
terse holds. PASS

one level where the register question carries the `lang-function-over-inventory`

|---|---|
| register | 65.8% |
| fidelity | 97.4% |

- Replies passing **both** axes first try: **63.2%** (24 of 38 scored) — gate is 85%. **FAIL**
- After one bounce: **84.2%** — gate is 95%. **FAIL**
- Bounce recovery: 8 of 14 bounced replies passed both axes on the rewrite (57.1%).

consistent: bolded headers, labelled bullets (`Cause:` / `Fix:`), roll-call lists and
full report grammar. The replies are accurate and well organised; they just do not
read like a message a colleague would type.

## What this says for the release

Recording this run does not certify E8 or E9. Two things need a decision before
v0.2.0 ships:

1. **The conciseness levels do not currently do anything measurable.** Either the
   contract needs to make the per-level target explicit enough that the rewriter
   varies output length, or the bands need to be re-derived from what the contract
   actually produces.
2. **The rewrite pass reaches for bullets by default**, and both failing evals trace
   back to that. The register axis will keep failing until the contract pushes short
   plain sentences ahead of structured lists for ordinary answers.

The anti-loss gate failing on 5 of 60 pairs is the more serious of the two findings,
since the anti-loss invariant is documented as outranking the bands.
